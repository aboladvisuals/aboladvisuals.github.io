A WhatsApp ordering and bookings assistant for a Hull bakery and catering business

A WhatsApp Cloud API bot for WHOIAM, Blessing’s bakery, confectionery, Nigerian food and catering business in Hull. The chat stays warm, fast and useful when she cannot reply herself: rule-based flows take the menu, orders and event bookings, and a short-memory assistant covers everything else.

Problem

Blessing runs WHOIAM from Hull — cakes, Nigerian food, small chops and catering for parties, weddings and owambe. Customers already message her on WhatsApp, often in English, Pidgin or Yoruba, and they expect a quick, human reply. When she is baking or delivering, the chat goes quiet, and informal back-and-forth is easy to drop. She needed a presence on WhatsApp that could answer everyday questions, take orders and event enquiries, and only bring her in when a human really had to step in.

Solution

I built a WhatsApp Cloud API bot that sounds like the business, not like a call centre.

  • Rule-based flows handle the menu, prices, delivery, payments, orders and catering bookings, so figures and payment details can never be invented.
  • A short-memory OpenAI assistant covers everything else: greetings, banter, and questions in English, Pidgin or Yoruba.
  • Completed orders (WHO-####) and event requests (EVT-####) are written to a Google Sheet.
  • Payment is upfront by Revolut or bank transfer; customers send a screenshot in the chat and Blessing confirms.
  • Handoff: if she wants to take over a conversation, she types #me to pause the bot and #bot to hand it back.

The bot is hosted on Render’s free tier and kept awake by UptimeRobot pinging GET / every five minutes.

My role

I designed, built and deployed the project end to end: the Express webhook, Meta Cloud API integration, order and booking flows, the AI voice and safety rules, Google Sheet storage, and production hosting. I also wrote the system prompt, tested real chats, and tightened the replies so they stay short, plain-text and suitable for WhatsApp.

Architecture

WhatsApp Cloud API  ─▶  Express webhook (HMAC signature on the raw body)
                           │
                ┌──────────┴──────────┐
                │ Menu, order, book   │  rule-based flows
                │ (prices & payments) │  business.js, replies.js,
                │                     │  orders.js, bookings.js
                └──────────┬──────────┘
                           │ otherwise
                      OpenAI gpt-4o-mini
                           │
         Completed WHO-#### / EVT-####  ─▶  Google Sheet (Apps Script doPost)

The split is deliberate. Once prices and payment instructions live in code, it is safe to let a model handle the leftover conversation. The model never sets a price, a date, a discount or a bank detail. It mirrors the customer’s language, keeps replies to one or two sentences, and only hands off to Blessing for payment confirmation, complaints or a custom quote. Each number keeps the last ten messages, with max_tokens at 150 and temperature at 0.8, so the chat stays brief and a little more natural.

Technologies

Node.js, Express (ES modules), Meta WhatsApp Cloud API, OpenAI gpt-4o-mini, Google Sheets with Apps Script, Render and UptimeRobot.

Implementation highlights

  • Rules before the model: I started with a thin Express webhook, then added a numbered menu and the order flow before bringing in any model. That order mattered.
  • Model choice: I first tried Google Gemini on the free tier. It was a poor fit: 503s and 404s, and stiff replies. I switched to OpenAI gpt-4o-mini through the official SDK, rewrote the system prompt around a warm Nigerian WhatsApp voice, and required plain-text URLs so Revolut links no longer arrived as Markdown.
  • Storage without a key file: a Google Apps Script web app (doPost plus a shared secret) instead of a service-account key. The bot POSTs each order and booking; Blessing can open the sheet like any other spreadsheet.
  • Hosting: Render’s free tier, with UptimeRobot to stop the service spinning down.

Challenges and fixes

  • ES module require error. The project is ES modules ("type": "module" in package.json). An early crash came from CommonJS require in that setup. I moved the app onto consistent import / export and the server started cleanly.
  • Webhook signature verification. Meta signs every POST /webhook with X-Hub-Signature-256. Express has to see the raw body, not a parsed JSON object, before the HMAC can be checked. I capture req.rawBody, sign it with APP_SECRET, and compare the hashes with crypto.timingSafeEqual, rejecting anything that does not match.
  • Gemini 503s and 404s. Free-tier Gemini was unreliable and the tone was off. Switching to gpt-4o-mini, tightening the prompt, and adding a safe fallback when the API errors (the bot sends a short default message instead of failing silently) made the chat stable enough for real testing.
  • Live-chat polish. Payment links had to be pasted as plain URLs, not Markdown, and image messages needed a dedicated “screenshot received” reply so a payment photo was not ignored.

Results

I tested the bot with real WhatsApp chats on 27 November 2026 (Europe/London). It held Pidgin and Yoruba banter (including an owambe for 100 guests, with ofada and ata dindin), quoted small-chops prices from the menu rules rather than inventing them, sent the Revolut link as a plain URL, and wrote both a test order and a test booking as new rows in the Sheet. Replies arrived in about a second (logs showed roughly 0.5–1.1s).

I have not published revenue or volume metrics for this project.

Limitations and next steps

  • The bot still runs on a test number. Coexistence — the bot and Blessing’s WhatsApp Business app sharing her real number — needs AboladLabs to become a Meta Tech Provider; business verification is pending.
  • Yoruba replies still need tightening: Yoruba in is sometimes answered in Pidgin.
  • In-progress sessions are not yet persisted, so a restart can reset a mid-flow order.
  • Possible later work includes automatic payment checks, owner alerts, and a white-label version for other businesses.

What this project demonstrates

  • Guarded applied AI: a model is used for tone and language, with prices, dates, discounts and payment details kept out of its reach.
  • WhatsApp Cloud API integration: a production Express webhook with raw-body HMAC verification and a documented handoff (#me / #bot).
  • Operational flow design: menu, order and catering booking paths that write completed work to a spreadsheet the business owner can open.
  • Practical hosting: a free-tier deploy kept awake, with fallbacks when an upstream API fails.
